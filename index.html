    Line Rider is a free, browser-based game that blends creativity with physics-based gameplay. Your task is to draw a track for a small sled rider and watch them ride along it at high speed.While the concept is simple, the physics system requires careful design. Poorly placed lines can cause crashes, while smooth and well-planned tracks create satisfying runs.

    You can test your track at any time using the built-in playback feature, making it easy to adjust and improve your design. This has led to a strong community of creators who build detailed and visually impressive tracks, with some creations resembling digital art rather than just gameplay.

    About Line Rider

    Line Rider was created by Boštjan Čadež in September 2006. The game is essentially a free-form track editor where you draw lines for a sled rider to follow, then press play to watch the path unfold based on physics.The idea is simple, but the results can be surprisingly complex. Gravity and momentum affect every line you draw, so smooth designs lead to clean rides, while rough tracks often cause crashes.

    The game became widely popular for its creative potential. Players began sharing their creations online, especially on YouTube, where detailed and synchronized tracks gained attention. One standout creator is TechDawg, known for building intricate tracks that match music, such as the well-known “TD & Sons” inspired by Van Halen’s Jump.

    Gameplay

    Line Rider is simple to start but gets challenging as your designs become more complex. You begin with a blank canvas and draw tracks for Bosh, the sled rider, to follow.

    Your goal is to create smooth, rideable paths that keep the rider moving without crashing. The game uses realistic physics, so angles, curves, and speed all matter. Sharp turns or poorly connected lines can cause the rider to fall off.

    You can use different line types to shape your track:

    • Blue Lines – Standard riding surfaces
    • Red Lines – Speed boosts to increase momentum

    You can erase and adjust your design at any time, then press play to instantly test how the track performs. This trial-and-error system is key to improving your builds.

    As you progress, you can create more advanced tracks with jumps, loops, and detailed layouts. Some players even design synchronized runs with music and visuals, turning tracks into creative projects.

    How to Play Line Rider

    In Line Rider, your objective is to draw tracks that Bosh, the sled rider, can safely follow using realistic physics. You design slopes, curves, and jumps while making sure the track stays smooth and rideable.

    Start with simple lines to create basic hills, then gradually build more complex sections like loops and speed zones. Since the canvas is unlimited, you can create long and detailed tracks that evolve over time.There’s no traditional win condition the goal is to build tracks that work well and look creative. You can save your designs, share them, and keep improving your skills.

    Controls

    On Computer:

    • Mouse Drag – Draw lines
    • Toolbar – Select tools (pencil, line, eraser)
    • Play Button – Test your track
    • Zoom Controls – Adjust view
    • F Key – Jump to flag positions
    • Hold D + Drag – Move starting position

    On Mobile:

    • Touch & Drag – Draw lines
    • Tap Tools – Select drawing tools
    • Pinch – Zoom in/out
    • Two-Finger Drag – Move around the canvas

    The controls are simple, but mastering them takes practice as your track designs become more advanced.

    Tips and Tricks

    Want better tracks? Stop winging it—Line Rider punishes sloppy design. Use these properly:

    • Start Simple
      Build basic slopes and curves first. Jumping straight into loops usually ends in crashes.
    • Respect Physics
      Sharp angles = instant failure. Smooth transitions are non-negotiable.
    • Use Red Lines Smartly
      Acceleration lines aren’t decoration—place them where speed actually matters.
    • Test Constantly
      If you wait until the end of the test, you’ve already wasted time. Check sections as you build.
    • Plan Before Drawing
      Random lines don’t turn into good tracks. Think through the path first.
    • Learn the Tools
      Freehand for flow, straight lines for control. If you don’t use both, your tracks will look amateur.
    • Use Flags
      Set checkpoints to test specific sections instead of replaying the whole track every time.
    • Be Patient
      Good tracks take refinement. If you keep restarting instead of fixing problems, you won’t improve.

    Do this right, and your tracks stop being messy sketches and start looking like intentional designs.

    Blue lines create normal rideable paths, while red lines add speed and help the rider move through difficult sections.

    How can I save my tracks?

    Most versions let you save tracks locally or export them to share with others.

    Start simple, test often, and use speed lines carefully for more complex parts.

    How long can tracks be?
